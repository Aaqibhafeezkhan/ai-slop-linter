# Should `dash` fail the build by default?

Generated by `node bench/dash.mjs`. Do not edit by hand; CI fails when this file does not match a fresh run.

## The question

`dash` used to report every dash as an `error`, so one dash in a file failed the run (exit 1) whatever the rest of the file looked like. The topic is contested: readers argue that people have always used the em dash. This file measures what each alternative does on the committed corpus (`bench/corpus`), nothing more.

## Files that fail, by policy

A file fails when it has an error-severity finding or its score is above the limit. Limit 10 is the default; 3 is what this repository sets in `.slop.json`.

| policy | human, limit 10 | machine, limit 10 | human, limit 3 | machine, limit 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| every dash is an error (before this decision) | 4 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 4 of 5 | 5 of 5 |
| em and en dash are errors, ASCII ` - ` and `--` are warnings (today) | 2 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 2 of 5 | 5 of 5 |
| every dash is a warning | 0 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 0 of 5 | 5 of 5 |
| dash is off | 0 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 0 of 5 | 5 of 5 |

Human files are texts written before 2021. Machine files are unedited model output. Lower is better for the first and third columns; higher is better for the second and fourth, if failing on model output is what you want.

Under the earlier policy (every dash an error) these human files failed at the default limit:

- `human/darwin-origin-of-species.md`
- `human/douglass-narrative.md`
- `human/pep-257-docstrings.md`
- `human/pep-8-style-guide.md`

## What this does not show

- 5 human files and 5 model files. Some of the human files are more than a century and a half old, and none is a modern README.
- It does not measure how readers react. Whether a failing build over one dash costs more goodwill than it saves is a judgement about people, not a number here.
- It does not say a file was written by a model. It counts a character.

## Decision

Decided on 2 November 2026: a real em dash (U+2014) or en dash used as a dash stays an `error`, so it still fails a run. A spaced ASCII hyphen (` - `) or a double hyphen (`--`) is a `warning`: it counts towards the score and fails a file only when the score is above the limit.

Reason: on public READMEs written before 2021, most ASCII hits were a name followed by a tagline or a key followed by its action, which people wrote long before models did. The real em dash remains the tell the rule was written for.

This is a choice made on the evidence above, not a claim that the question is settled for readers. The corpus is small (see above), and the choice can be reversed in `src/rules/dashes.ts`. If the error is unwelcome in your repository, turn the rule off with `--ignore dash`.
